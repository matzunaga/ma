Why the light moves this way

The leading explanation for eye movements in EMDR is working memory: following a moving target while holding a memory uses the same limited mental workspace, so the memory tends to lose vividness and charge. Lab studies usually use one full left-right-left cycle per second. One study compared a slow 0.8 and a fast 1.2 cycles per second; faster movement loaded working memory more. Ma runs gentler than the lab: Standard is half a cycle per second, one pass each second, like common 60 beats per minute bilateral audio tracks; Slow is 0.4 and Fast is 0.6. Calm, at a quarter cycle per second, gives each pass two full seconds.

Why the drum follows the eyes

In lab comparisons, eye movements taxed working memory more than alternating tones. So the pendulum does the main work and the drum marks each side. The drum is synthesized, with overtones placed where a real circular drumhead puts them.

Why these colors

In Valdez and Mehrabian's color study, brightness raised pleasure and lowered arousal, saturation raised arousal, and blue, purple-blue and blue-green were among the most pleasant hues. So the page is bright, low-saturation washi paper painted in a single soft indigo ink, like a sumi-e wash painting; even the strikes are marked in that same ink. Night mode keeps the same single ink on deep indigo rather than black, which that study found the most arousing neutral.

Why the wind

Brown noise drops off steeply in the high frequencies and sounds like distant surf or deep wind. Pink noise keeps equal energy in every octave, so it's a little brighter, closer to steady rain, but still far softer than the hiss of white noise. It plays equally in both ears, so it never blurs the left and right of the drum. It swells and fades over tens of seconds, never in time with the beat, and the grass follows the same swell.

What this is for

On your own, this is a regulation tool: settling, grounding, slowing down. Working through trauma with bilateral stimulation belongs with a trained therapist.
